# Azure App Service Acquisition and Usage Analysis

**Lead Data Scientist:** Ryan Bouchard

## Business Problem

Describe how Azure App Service accounts were acquired and what account-day
usage is observed in the supplied extract. The goal is to identify patterns
worth validating with the product team and define useful next steps. This is
descriptive analysis, not a causal evaluation or validated retention study.

## Data Sources and Grain

| Source | Grain and coverage |
|---|---|
| `Case_AccountData.csv` | One row per account; segment and development-language categories. |
| `Case_AccountCreation.csv` | One creation record per account; supplied dates Feb 1–27, 2022. |
| `Case_UsageData.csv` | One row per observed account-day; dates Feb 1–Apr 30, 2022. |

Join on `AccountID`, respecting each source grain. The acquisition cohort
contains 977 unique accounts. Use source CSVs as-is; do not modify them.

## Metric Definitions and Limitations

- **Acquisition composition / daily and weekly timing:** account count and
  share of all 977 cohort accounts. Sunday-start weeks Feb 1–5 and Feb 27 are
  partial buckets.
- **Calendar observed coverage:** on date *t*, distinct accounts with an
  observed usage record that date divided by cohort accounts created on or
  before *t*. Eligible count increases until Feb 27; the extract ends Apr 30.
  Pooled calendar coverage divides all observed rows by all eligible
  account-dates across Feb 1–Apr 30.
- **Equal account-age coverage:** for each integer age 0–62, distinct accounts
  with an observed usage record at that age divided by all 977 accounts.
  Age 0 is the inclusive creation date; every account has the full 63-day
  window in the extract.
- **Per-account observed-day count/rate:** distinct observed usage dates in
  ages 0–62; rate is count divided by 63.
- An absent account-day means **no row observed in this extract**, not verified
  inactivity. Usage extract end limits calendar follow-up for later creation
  cohorts. The fixed age window addresses unequal follow-up only for ages
  0–62. There is no established retention event; results are descriptive, not
  causal or inferential.

## Key Findings

- Customer type: SMB 625/977 (63.97%), Enterprise 242/977 (24.77%),
  Credit_Program 110/977 (11.26%). Language: unknown 362/977 (37.05%),
  dotnet 266 (27.23%), node 119 (12.18%), python 105 (10.75%), php 84
  (8.60%), and java 41 (4.20%).
- Daily acquisition peaked Feb 7 at 87 accounts. Sunday-start buckets held
  174, 324, 239, 234, and 6 accounts; first and last are partial.
- There are 44,352 observed rows across 75,386 eligible calendar account-days
  (58.83% pooled); daily coverage was 28/28 on Feb 1 and 407/977 on Apr 30.
- Equal-age coverage was 977/977 at day 0, 591/977 at day 30, and 421/977 at
  day 62; mean age-day coverage was 62.58%. Per-account observed days in
  ages 0–62: median 35, mean 39.42, range 1–63.
- None of these metrics establish non-use, churn, retention, or future
  behavior. See the notebook for actionable interpretation and validation
  questions.
